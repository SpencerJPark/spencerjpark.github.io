Panera's Bun Bun tutorial, rebuilt in three days

Freelance video producer for Brand Revolution

Panera was sending Bun Bun crochet kits to creators to build hype and draw people into its locations for craft meetups. The kit hinged on a tutorial video as the only instructions, the video recieved from the vendor was ineffective. I was brought in by Brand Revolution to create the current tutorial.

The footage, shot at the vendor's factory in China, was rough and far from a finished product. The last half of the instructions, the hardest part, was missing entirely.

So I rebuilt it. I got the factory's original script, learned basic crochet so I could recognize every stitch, and rewrote the instructions step by step. From there, I handled everything: color grading, editing, subtitles, client revisions, YouTube setup with chapters, and the QR code printed in every kit.

Cutting scope to hold the date We had a month, with a voice actor lined up. Then the QR code had to reach the factory before the kits went to print, and the timeline dropped to three days. I pitched a short link that would have kept the original schedule and allowed client revisions right up until launch. When the client declined because they needed a direct YouTube link, I pivoted again and cut scope. I swapped the voice actor for an ElevenLabs voice and delivered on time.

Finding what Just Poke's recipes actually cost

Technical business consultant (contract) for Conscious Hospitality Group, a multi-brand restaurant franchisor

Conscious Hospitality brought me in to fix Just Poke's backend data. I pulled pricing from four separate systems, their POS, CRM, inventory, and fulfillment, into one place leadership could actually use.

Digging through it, I caught a bigger problem. Nobody knew what their recipes actually cost. They'd reworked the whole menu, but the costs were still tied to the old recipes. On top of that, the new recipes were written by volume, while ingredients were bought by weight. There was no way to get the real numbers from their data.

I also vetted technology vendors for them, so they could choose tools that would keep their data clean going forward.

Getting the real numbers I went to the source. I set up a morning with a Just Poke franchisee and helped them prep. As we made all 26 recipes, from the main bowls to the shared sauces, I measured every ingredient in the units their vendors sell it in. Then I built a cost calculator for leadership. They can change the price of one ingredient, like soy sauce, and instantly see which dishes slip out of margin, or test a menu change before rolling it out.

Reworking a factory's order assembly line

Inventory / project manager at Bramble Berry, a handcraft supply eCommerce company

At Bramble Berry, a handcraft supply company, I reorganized how orders were assembled and directed the fulfillment, production, and distribution teams. I built the metrics we used to track the warehouse, used them to make order assembly more efficient, and trained the department leads.

To measure it, I timed order assembly before and after the changes and compared the averages. Orders were getting built 13.6% faster, which meant the same team could get more out the door each day. That paid off when COVID hit and sales jumped, because it helped us keep up with the spike.

Three events for WinStar World Casino

Event operations for Three Brothers, Inc., a promotional products company

WinStar is one of the largest casinos in the world, and I worked their VIP events through Three Brothers, Inc. My job was to plan the flow, work the crowd, and keep people moving while making sure every guest left happy.

Black Friday shopping cart dash

The shopping cart dash went so well that WinStar brought us back three years in a row, until COVID shut it down. Every hour for 12 hours, three VIP guests got their turn to race a shopping cart through a mock store, with an average haul of about $9,300 each. The final event in 2019 set the record for the highest single-day coin-in of WinStar's history at $168M.

Tumi jacket fittings

For the Tumi jacket event, I was part of the team that ran 1,400 fittings. The challenge was cadence: bringing guests in, getting them happy, and moving them along. The jackets also ran smaller than expected, so some sizes ran low. When that happened, I made sure guests still had good options, like ordering one in their size or picking out a jacket for their spouse, so the fittings stayed fun even when stock didn't cooperate.

New Year's Eve gifts

For New Year's, our brief was simple: give WinStar's VIP guests an "ooh and ahh." The ooh came that evening, with sparkling crystal sets. The ahh came the next morning, when guests slipped into a fresh pair of UGGs. I ran the fittings for all 900 pairs, so guests walked away in the right size and feeling taken care of.
