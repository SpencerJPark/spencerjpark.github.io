Panera's Bun Bun tutorial, rebuilt in three days

Freelance video producer for Brand Revolution

Panera sent Bun Bun crochet kits to creators to build hype and get them posting, with the goal of bringing people into Panera locations to hold craft meetups. The kit's only instructions were its tutorial video. The version from the factory in China was shot in poor lighting, so the hands looked gray. It had a robotic AI voiceover, and it was missing the last half of the instructions, the most complicated part.

I got the factory's original script so I had a starting point, then learned basic crochet so I could recognize the stitches and rewrite every step in order. I color graded the footage back to natural skin tones, and handled the edit, subtitles, client revisions, YouTube setup with chapter timestamps, and the QR code printed in every kit.

Cutting scope to hold the date The plan was a month, with a voice actor already lined up. Then the QR code had to reach the factory before the kits went to print. I offered a short link, which would have kept the original timeline and let the client update the video later. When they needed a direct YouTube link, I cut scope instead: I replaced the voice actor with an ElevenLabs voice and delivered in three days.

Finding what Just Poke's recipes actually cost

Technical business consultant (contract) for Conscious Hospitality Group, a multi-brand restaurant group

I was brought in to clean up the backend data for Just Poke, pulling pricing from their POS, CRM, inventory, and fulfillment systems into one reporting layer for leadership.

While going through it, I found that nobody knew what any recipe cost. The menu had been reworked, but the costs were still based on the old recipes. The new recipes were also written in volumes, while ingredients were bought by weight, so there was no way to calculate the real cost from the data they had.

I also interviewed technology vendors to help them choose tools that would keep their data cleaner going forward.

Getting the real numbers I asked to be put in touch with a Just Poke franchisee and scheduled a morning to help them prep. As we made all 26 recipes, from the main bowls to the sauces shared between them, I measured every ingredient in the same units their vendors sell it in. Then I built a spreadsheet that works like a calculator: leadership can change an ingredient's price, like soy sauce, and see right away which dishes are slipping out of margin, or test a menu change before making it.

Three events for WinStar World Casino

Event operations for Three Brothers, Inc., a promotional products company

WinStar is one of the largest casinos in the world. Each event was different, but the job was the same: plan the flow, work the crowd, and keep people moving while making sure they left happy.

Black Friday shopping cart dash

A game-show-style event that went so well WinStar brought us back to run it three years in a row, until COVID stopped it. We put three contestants an hour through it for 12 hours straight, 36 in all, and the average haul was about $9,300 in retail value. The final day in 2019 was the highest single-day coin-in in WinStar's history.

Tumi jacket fittings

1,400 jacket fittings. The challenge was cadence: how fast we could bring guests in, get them a jacket they were happy with, and move them along. We refined the flow and the signage until it ran smoothly. When the jackets ran small against their size chart, I solved shortages on the spot with reorders, or a jacket sized for the guest's spouse.

New Year's Eve gifts

The brief was "ooh and ahh." The ooh came from crystal sets, and the ahh came from how comfortable the UGGs felt. We ran all the fitting services, including shoe fittings for the UGGs, and handled the on-site hand-out.
